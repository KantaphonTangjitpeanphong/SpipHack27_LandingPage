What is SPIP Hack?

SPIP Hack is a student-led, cross-school innovation competition where students work in teams to identify real-world problems and develop creative, practical solutions.

Theme: The City of Tomorrow.

Basic Run-down

SPIP Hack

SPIP Hack 2027 is split into 2 rounds:

  • Round 1 — Idea Submission, online
  • Round 2 — Innovation Final, on-site

Held at Satit Prasarnmit International Programme (SPIP), in English.

Basic Run-down

Rounds

Round 1 — Idea Submission (Online)

  • Teams submit a proposal and a short video pitch
  • Judged blindly by SPIP judges — top 10 selected

Round 2 — Innovation Final (On-Site)

  • Mentor feedback, then refine your idea
  • 5-min pitch + 5-min Q&A
  • Judged by external professionals & judges

Participants and winners receive certificates.

Event Format

Hackathon

An event where participants come up with a creative and innovative solution to a given prompt.

They then pitch their ideas to a panel of judges, who score each idea and choose a winner.

Basic Guidelines

Teams

  • 2–4 members per team, Year 9 / Grade 8 / M.2 and above
  • No specific subject background required
  • Cross-school teams are encouraged
  • Open to any school, max 3 teams per school
  • A multi-school team counts towards the quota of both schools

Round 1 · Idea Submission · Online

From idea to innovation

Every team starts here. Form a team, pick a challenge, and submit your idea online — no travel needed. The top 10 teams go through to the Innovation Final at SPIP.

  1. 01

    Form your team

    2–4 students, Year 9 / Grade 8 / M.2 and above. No specific subject background is required. Open to international, Thai, bilingual / EP, demonstration and other eligible secondary schools.

    Cross-school teams are encouraged — bring together different skills, perspectives and experiences. Each school can enter up to 3 teams; a multi-school team counts towards both schools' quota.

  2. 02

    Pick your challenge

    The theme is The City of Tomorrow. Choose one area — or combine ideas, or invent your own.

    • Health & WellbeingHow can we make cities healthier and improve quality of life?
    • Sustainability & EnvironmentHow can we reduce environmental impact and use resources more responsibly?
    • Mobility & Urban LivingHow can we make it easier, safer, and better to live and move around cities?
    • Future TechnologyHow can emerging technology help solve real-world urban problems?
  3. 03

    Submit your idea

    Submit online via Google Forms, in English. Your submission covers:

    • Team & school info
    • Problem statement
    • Why the problem matters
    • Proposed solution
    • Intended users / beneficiaries
    • Basic implementation concept
    • Potential impact
    • 3–5 minute video pitch

    We're looking for promising ideas and strong reasoning, not finished businesses.

  4. 04

    Get selected

    Ideas are judged blindly by SPIP judges — judges don't see team or school names. The top 10 teams go through to the Innovation Final. Each criterion is worth 20%:

    • Problem significance & understanding
    • Innovation & originality
    • Feasibility
    • Potential impact
    • Communication & proposal quality
