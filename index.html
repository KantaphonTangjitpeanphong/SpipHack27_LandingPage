Introducing

What is SPIP Hack?

SPIP's first-ever student-led hackathon — a cross-school innovation pitching competition.

Theme: City of Tomorrow.

Basic Run-down

SPIP Hack

A student-led hackathon split into 2 stages:

  • The Pre-Hack: idea submission, online
  • The Hackathon: a two-day, on-site innovation event for finalists

Held at Satit Prasarnmit International Programme (SPIP), in English.

Basic Run-down

Rounds

Pre-Hack (Online)

  • Teams submit an initial proposal
  • Scored by a panel of judges, with school affiliation hidden

Hackathon (On-Site, two days)

  • Target of 15 finalist teams
  • Day 1: mentoring and building, ending in Project Lock
  • Day 2: presentations, Q&A, judging and awards

Participants and winners receive certificates.

Event Format

Hackathon

An event where participants come up with a creative and innovative solution to a given prompt.

They then pitch their ideas to a panel of judges, who score each idea and choose a winner.

Basic Guidelines

Teams

  • 2–4 members per team
  • Members from multiple schools and year groups are allowed
  • Open to students in Year 9 / Grade 8 / M.2 or above at international, bilingual, English-program or Thai secondary schools
  • Up to 3 teams from the same school can qualify for the Hackathon

Four directions · One better city

Start with a better question.

Pick the part of city life you want to change. Find what's broken, then imagine what could work better.

  • 01

    Health & Wellbeing

    How can we make cities healthier and improve quality of life?

    Public health · mental wellbeing · healthcare access · ageing populations · disease prevention

  • 02

    Sustainability & Environment

    How can we cut waste and pollution and build cities that can handle a changing climate?

    Waste · air quality · water · energy · climate resilience · sustainable materials

  • 03

    Mobility & Urban Living

    How can everyone get around the city, and live in it, more easily and safely?

    Public transport · accessibility · traffic · urban spaces · housing · safety

  • 04

    Future Technology

    How can emerging technology help solve real-world urban problems?

    AI · sensors · robotics · smart infrastructure · digital systems · emerging technologies

Examples, not restrictions — combine areas or explore your own.

Round 1 · Pre-Hack · Idea Submission

Every team starts here

Form a team, take on the theme, and submit your proposal online — no travel needed. The highest-ranked proposals earn a place at the two-day Hackathon at SPIP.

  1. 01

    Form your team

    2–4 members per team, open to students in Year 9 / Grade 8 / M.2 or above at an international, bilingual, English-program or Thai secondary school. Teammates can come from different schools and year groups.

  2. 02

    Take on the theme

    This year's theme is City of Tomorrow. Your team answers one question:

    How can we use innovation to make cities healthier, more sustainable, and better places to live?
  3. 03

    Submit your proposal online

    Write up your idea as an initial proposal and submit it online, in English, by 13 February 2027 (tentative). Submission details will be announced when registration opens — follow @spip.hack (opens in a new tab) so you don't miss it.

  4. 04

    Judging & selection

    A panel of judges scores every eligible proposal with the same Pre-Hack rubric, and judges don't see which school you're from while they score. Proposals are ranked, with a target of 15 finalist teams (the number may be adjusted). Up to 3 teams from the same school can qualify — a limit applied only after ranking. Finalists are announced on 21 February 2027 (tentative) — see how they're chosen.
