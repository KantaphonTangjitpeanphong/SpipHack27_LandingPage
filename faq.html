The event

What is SPIP Hack?

SPIP Hack 2027 is a student-led, cross-school innovation competition at Satit Prasarnmit International Programme (SPIP), Bangkok, where secondary students identify real-world urban problems and develop innovative solutions to them.

Teams enter the Pre-Hack with a problem and an initial idea. The Hackathon then gives finalists the time, mentorship and space to turn that idea into a stronger solution.

What is this year's theme?

City of Tomorrow (เมืองแห่งอนาคต): developing innovative solutions that make cities healthier, more sustainable, more accessible and better places to live. Your project should tackle a real-world problem linked to that challenge.

What topics can our project cover?

Possible areas include:

  • Health & Wellbeing — public health, mental wellbeing, healthcare accessibility, healthy environments, ageing populations, disease prevention
  • Sustainability & Environment — waste, air quality, water, energy, climate resilience, sustainable materials
  • Mobility & Urban Living — public transport, accessibility, traffic, urban spaces, housing, safety
  • Future Technology — AI, sensors, robotics, smart infrastructure, digital systems, emerging technologies

These are examples, not restrictions. You can combine areas or explore another topic, as long as your project addresses the central challenge.

How does the competition work?

There are two stages:

  • Pre-Hack (Idea Submission) — every team submits an initial proposal, scored with the Pre-Hack rubric. Selected teams advance to the Hackathon.
  • Hackathon (Two-Day Innovation Event) — finalists spend two days on-site getting mentor feedback, refining their projects and presenting their final solutions to a panel of judges.

Along the way, your project moves from problem identification → research → ideation → solution development → feedback → refinement → presentation.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You don't need prior experience in coding, engineering or entrepreneurship, and a fully functional prototype isn't required. See the judging rubric for what judges look for.

What language is the competition in?

English — for both the proposal and the presentation.

Teams & eligibility

Who can take part?

Students enrolled in Year 9 / Grade 8 / M.2 or above (or equivalent) at an international, bilingual, English-program or Thai secondary school.

How big can a team be?

Each team has 2–4 students, so you'll need at least one teammate.

Can teammates come from different schools or year groups?

Yes to both — teams can mix year groups and mix schools, as long as every member meets the eligibility requirements.

What is an SPIP Team?

A team is an SPIP Team if at least 50% of its registered members are current SPIP students. Any other team is an External Team. Only officially registered members are counted.

The classification is used when finalists are selected — see Are SPIP Teams guaranteed a place? under Registration & Pre-Hack.

Can we change our team members?

Your team should be finalised before the Pre-Hack submission deadline. Any change after you submit needs approval from the organisers.

Your project

What does our project need to do?

Every project must:

  • Address a genuine problem
  • Relate to the City of Tomorrow theme
  • Propose an identifiable solution
  • Identify its intended users or beneficiaries
  • Consider how the solution could be implemented
  • Explain its potential impact
  • Be your team's own work
What kind of solution can we create?

Projects can include physical products, digital products, AI systems, sensors, scientific solutions, services, business models, environmental solutions, or policy and urban-design concepts — or a combination of these.

Do we need a working prototype?

No — a working prototype isn't required. If you do have one, you can bring it to the Hackathon: prototypes, models and demonstrations can all be part of your final presentation.

Can we use AI tools?

AI tools may be used where the competition permits. Your team stays responsible for the accuracy of your work, understanding your own project, verifying information, your final claims and conclusions, and originality.

The final AI-use requirements will be announced before the Pre-Hack opens.

Does everything have to be our own work?

Yes. Everything you submit must be your team's own work. You must not:

  • Plagiarise
  • Present someone else's idea as your own
  • Falsify research, data, results or sources
  • Misrepresent testing
  • Deliberately mislead the judges

You can use publicly available research, technologies, software and other legitimate resources, as long as you acknowledge them. Serious violations may lead to removal or disqualification.

Can we build on an existing or previous project?

Only if you've made substantial development or modification — this applies to existing projects and previous competition entries alike. You must be able to explain what's new or different about this submission.

Who owns our idea?

Your team keeps ownership of your original ideas and materials, unless otherwise agreed. You're responsible for getting permission to use anyone else's images, videos, music, software, data, research or other intellectual property, and for acknowledging your sources.

Registration & Pre-Hack

How do we register?

Registration hasn't opened yet. Follow @spip.hack (opens in a new tab) on Instagram — we'll announce it there first.

What do we submit for the Pre-Hack?

An initial proposal, in English, submitted through the official submission system. It should include:

  • Team name, school(s), team members and year groups
  • Your problem statement, and why the problem matters
  • Your proposed solution
  • Its intended users or beneficiaries
  • Your initial approach — how the idea might work
  • Its potential impact

Before you submit, check that your sources are acknowledged and the work is original.

When is the deadline, and what are the key dates?

The Pre-Hack submission deadline is 13 February 2027. The full timeline Tentative:

  • 13 Feb — Pre-Hack submission deadline
  • 14–20 Feb — Pre-Hack judging
  • 21 Feb — Finalist teams announced
  • 22–28 Feb — Finalist briefing and attendance confirmations
  • 1–4 Mar — Final event preparations
  • 6–7 Mar — The Hackathon (Two-Day Innovation Event)

Late submissions may not be accepted, so don't leave it to the last minute.

How is the Pre-Hack judged?

Every eligible proposal is scored out of 100 with the same rubric:

  • Problem Understanding & Significance — 30%
  • Originality / Innovation — 25%
  • Potential Impact — 20%
  • Initial Solution Direction — 15%
  • Reasoning & Clarity — 10%

Judges don't see which school you're from while they score, and you're not expected to have solved feasibility yet. The Pre-Hack rubric explains each criterion.

How many teams reach the Hackathon?

The target is 15 finalist teams — the capacity of the two-day Hackathon. The final number may be adjusted if necessary.

Is there a limit on teams from the same school?

Yes — a maximum of 3 teams from the same school can qualify for the Hackathon. Every eligible proposal is scored and ranked first; the school limit is applied only after that.

Are SPIP Teams guaranteed a place?

No. SPIP Teams that rank among the qualifying teams get in the normal way, subject to the school limit. If no SPIP Team qualifies on its own, the highest-ranked eligible SPIP Team may be selected as an SPIP representative — only if it meets the required quality and eligibility standards — replacing the lowest-ranked qualifying team where necessary.

Merit decides the ranking; SPIP representation is a secondary consideration, not an automatic quota.

The Hackathon

When and where is the Hackathon?

The two-day Hackathon is planned for 6–7 March 2027 Tentative, on-site at SPIP (4th floor Mini Theatre) — 176 Sukhumvit 23, Wattana, Bangkok 10110. It's a short walk from BTS Asok and MRT Sukhumvit.

Do finalists need to attend in person?

Yes — the Hackathon is an on-site event. Finalist teams confirm attendance in the briefing week after finalists are announced, and should have any required forms submitted and equipment needs sent to the organisers before the event.

What happens over the two days?
  • Day 1 — Learn, Develop, Mentor, Build, Refine, Project Lock. Workshops, mentor sessions and technical development. All presentation materials and project files must be submitted by the end of Day 1 (Project Lock).
  • Day 2 — Present, Q&A, Judging, Awards. Teams present to the judges and answer their questions live, followed by deliberation and the awards ceremony.

The programme also includes keynote sessions.

What help will mentors give?

Mentors with relevant expertise may give feedback on problem definition, technical and scientific reasoning, feasibility, user needs, impact, innovation, evidence and presentation. You can then improve your solution, prototype or demonstration, reasoning, evidence, implementation strategy, business model, user experience and presentation.

Mentors provide guidance only — your team stays responsible for its own work and decisions.

How long is the final presentation?

The proposed format is a 5-minute presentation followed by 5 minutes of Q&A. The final time limit will be confirmed at the finalist briefing.

You can use slides, prototypes, models and demonstrations. Videos are allowed as supporting material, embedded in or included within your presentation.

How is the final judged?

A panel of judges scores each team out of 100 with a separate final rubric:

  • Problem Understanding — 15%
  • Innovation & Originality — 20%
  • Feasibility & Implementation — 20%
  • Potential Impact — 20%
  • Evidence & Reasoning — 10%
  • Presentation & Communication — 10%
  • Response to Q&A — 5%

The final rubric explains each criterion.

What equipment will be provided?

The competition may provide standard presentation equipment: a display or projector, a presentation computer, microphones, speakers, and internet access where available.

Don't assume specialised equipment will be there — if you need it, tell the organisers in advance. Keep backup copies of your important files.

Can we run a live experiment or demonstration?

Demonstrations are allowed, but you must not run dangerous experiments or demonstrations without prior approval. If your demo needs specialised equipment, let the organisers know in advance.

Judging & results

How do you keep judging fair?

Every team is judged against the same criteria. Judges must stay fair, impartial and objective and declare any significant conflict of interest — a judge can be excluded from scoring a team where one exists. In the Pre-Hack, school affiliation is hidden from judges during initial scoring.

What happens if teams tie?

These criteria are considered, in order:

  • Innovation & Originality
  • Feasibility & Implementation
  • Potential Impact
  • Problem Understanding

The organisers may set further tie-breaking steps if required.

Can we appeal a result?

No. Judges' decisions on scores, rankings, finalists and awards are final and cannot be appealed, and scores won't be changed because a team asks. Administrative, safeguarding, eligibility, misconduct and safety matters remain under the authority of SPIP and its school procedures.

Prizes & certificates

What can we win?

The Proposed main awards total ฿10,000:

  • 1st place — ฿5,000
  • 2nd place — ฿3,000
  • 3rd place — ฿2,000

Special awards may also be introduced, such as Most Innovative, Best Technology Application, Best Sustainability Solution, Best Social Impact and Best Presentation. Final award categories and prizes will be announced before the final.

Does everyone get a certificate?

Yes — all participants and winners receive certificates.

Conduct, safety & media

What are the rules on behaviour?

Treat other participants, judges, mentors, staff and organisers with respect; follow competition and school rules, staff instructions and safety requirements; respect school property; and behave appropriately throughout the event.

Bullying or harassment, discrimination, intimidation, deliberate disruption, cheating, plagiarism, falsification, unsafe behaviour and damage to property are all prohibited. Serious violations may result in removal or disqualification.

Will we be photographed or filmed?

Photos and video may be taken during the event for educational, documentation and promotional purposes, subject to the school's policies and consent requirements.

Updates & help

Could the rules or dates change?

Yes. The rulebook is still a working draft, subject to school approval, and the dates are tentative. The organisers may also make reasonable changes where necessary — for example because of school requirements, safety, venue availability, scheduling, participant numbers or technical issues.

Changes that affect participants will be announced through official channels, and the latest approved rulebook always takes precedence over earlier drafts or informal information.
