Judging rubric

How you'll be judged

Your Pre-Hack proposal is scored out of 100 against five criteria, and finalists' presentations out of 100 against seven. Use this page to plan your proposal and your presentation — it's exactly what the judges will be looking for.

Round 1 · Pre-Hack Round 2 · Final

Draft Based on the Participant Rulebook (v3), which is still subject to school approval. Criteria and weights may change before registration opens. The final rubric will be posted here and on @spip.hack (opens in a new tab).

Score bands

For each criterion, judges pick the band that best fits your work, then give a score within it — shown as a share of that criterion's points.

  • Excellent 90–100%
  • Strong 70–89%
  • Developing 40–69%
  • Beginning below 40%

Round 1 · Pre-Hack · Idea Submission

Pre-Hack rubric

Every team submits an initial proposal through the official submission system, and every eligible proposal is scored with this same rubric. Judges don't see which school you're from while they score.

Your proposal should cover your problem statement, why the problem matters, your proposed solution, its intended users, your initial approach (how the idea might work) and its potential impact — plus your team name, school(s), members and year groups.

100 points across 5 criteria

  1. 01

    Problem Understanding & Significance

    How clearly you define the problem, its context, and why it matters for cities.

    30 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    A clearly defined, real city problem, with context and evidence showing why it matters and who it affects.
    Strong70–89%
    A clear, relevant problem with some context on why it matters.
    Developing40–69%
    The problem is broad or vague, or its importance is assumed rather than shown.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    The problem is unclear or not connected to the City of Tomorrow.
  2. 02

    Originality / Innovation

    How creative and new the idea is, and whether it makes meaningful use of innovation or technology.

    25 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    A fresh idea, or a clever new twist on an existing one, that uses innovation or technology in a meaningful way.
    Strong70–89%
    A creative idea that clearly improves on what already exists.
    Developing40–69%
    A familiar idea with only small changes, or technology added without a clear purpose.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Copies an existing solution, or shows little original thinking.
  3. 03

    Potential Impact

    The benefits your idea could bring to users, communities, the environment and society.

    20 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    Clear, believable benefits, with a sense of who would benefit and how much.
    Strong70–89%
    Clear benefits, though who benefits or by how much is less developed.
    Developing40–69%
    Some benefit, but it is small, vague or loosely linked to the problem.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Little or no benefit is explained.
  4. 04

    Initial Solution Direction

    How clear and promising your proposed solution is, and your first reasoning for how it might work. You don't need to have solved feasibility yet.

    15 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    A clear, promising solution with sensible first reasoning for how it could work and who would use it.
    Strong70–89%
    A clear solution with some reasoning about how it might work.
    Developing40–69%
    The solution is vague, or how it tackles the problem is unclear.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    No clear solution, or it doesn't address the problem.
  5. 05

    Reasoning & Clarity

    How clearly the proposal is organised, and the information that supports it.

    10 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    Well organised and easy to follow, with supporting information and acknowledged sources behind key points.
    Strong70–89%
    Clear overall, with minor gaps in organisation or support.
    Developing40–69%
    Hard to follow in places, or claims lack support.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Disorganised, or very little supporting information.

From Pre-Hack to the final

  • Proposals are ranked by their Pre-Hack score. The target is 15 finalist teams — the capacity of the two-day Hackathon — though the number may be adjusted if necessary.
  • A maximum of 3 teams from the same school can qualify. This limit is applied only after every proposal has been scored and ranked.
  • SPIP Teams (at least half of the registered members are current SPIP students) get no guaranteed places. If none ranks into the finalists, the highest-ranked eligible SPIP Team may replace the lowest-ranked qualifying team — only if it meets the required quality and eligibility standards. Merit comes first; this is not an automatic quota.

Round 2 · Two-Day Hackathon · Final

Final rubric

Finalists spend two days on-site at SPIP. Day 1 is for workshops, mentor sessions and building — all presentation materials and project files must be submitted by the end of the day (Project Lock). On Day 2, each team presents to the judges and answers their questions live. Finalists are scored with this separate final rubric.

The proposed format is a 5-minute presentation + 5-minute Q&A; the final time limit will be confirmed at the finalist briefing. You can use slides, prototypes, models and demonstrations. Videos are allowed as supporting material embedded or included in your presentation.

100 points across 7 criteria

  1. 01

    Problem Understanding

    How clearly you explain the problem, its context, why it matters and who it affects.

    15 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    A sharply defined problem, with clear context, a strong case for why it matters and a good grasp of the users affected.
    Strong70–89%
    A clear problem and users, with some context on why it matters.
    Developing40–69%
    The problem or the people affected are only loosely defined.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    The problem is unclear.
  2. 02

    Innovation & Originality

    How creative and original the solution is, and whether technology is used appropriately.

    20 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    A fresh, memorable solution that makes clear what sets it apart, with technology used where it genuinely helps.
    Strong70–89%
    A creative solution with a clear point of difference.
    Developing40–69%
    A familiar idea with only small changes.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Little original thinking.
  3. 03

    Feasibility & Implementation

    Whether it could work in practice: the technology, resources, scalability and an understanding of its limits.

    20 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    A realistic plan for building it, covering resources and scaling up, with an honest view of its limitations.
    Strong70–89%
    Mostly realistic, with some gaps in resources, scaling or limitations.
    Developing40–69%
    Possible in principle, but key practical questions are left open.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Unrealistic, or no thought given to how it would be built or used.
  4. 04

    Potential Impact

    The benefits for communities, whether environmental, health, social or economic, and how far it could scale.

    20 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    Convincing environmental, health, social or economic benefits, with a clear sense of scale.
    Strong70–89%
    Clear benefits, but less evidence of scale.
    Developing40–69%
    Benefits are stated more than shown.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Impact is unclear.
  5. 05

    Evidence & Reasoning

    The research, data and scientific and logical reasoning behind your claims, and how accurate they are.

    10 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    Accurate, well-sourced research and data, with sound reasoning behind each claim.
    Strong70–89%
    Good evidence for most claims, with minor gaps.
    Developing40–69%
    Limited evidence, or some reasoning doesn't hold up.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Claims are unsupported or inaccurate.
  6. 06

    Presentation & Communication

    How clear, organised and engaging your presentation is, including visuals and use of time.

    10 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    Clear, engaging and well organised, with strong visuals, and finishes on time.
    Strong70–89%
    Clear and organised, with minor issues in pacing or visuals.
    Developing40–69%
    Hard to follow in places, or over time.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Unclear, unprepared or well over time.
  7. 07

    Response to Q&A

    How well you understand your project, answer the judges' questions and defend your decisions.

    5 pts

    Excellent90–100%
    Confident, accurate answers that show deep understanding and justify the team's decisions.
    Strong70–89%
    Answers most questions well.
    Developing40–69%
    Some answers are vague or off topic.
    Beginningbelow 40%
    Struggles to answer questions.

Judging rules

  • Judges score every team against the same criteria. They must stay fair, impartial and objective, and declare any significant conflict of interest. A judge with a significant conflict of interest may be excluded from scoring that team.
  • If teams tie, these criteria are compared in order: Innovation & Originality, then Feasibility & Implementation, then Potential Impact, then Problem Understanding. The organisers may add further tie-breaking steps if needed.
  • Judges' decisions on scores, rankings, finalists and awards are final and cannot be appealed. Scores won't be changed because a team asks. Administrative, safeguarding, eligibility, misconduct and safety matters are handled by SPIP under school procedures.
  • Everything you submit must be your team's own work. Don't plagiarise, present someone else's idea as your own, falsify research, data, results or sources, misrepresent testing, or deliberately mislead the judges. Acknowledge the research, technology and resources you use. Serious violations can lead to removal or disqualification.
  • You can build on an earlier project or competition entry only if you've substantially developed or changed it, and you must be able to explain what's new.
  • AI tools may be used where the competition allows. Your team stays responsible for the accuracy, verification and originality of your work, including its final claims and conclusions, and must understand your own project. The final AI-use rules will be shared before the Pre-Hack opens.
